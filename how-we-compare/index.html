Most job boards answer: “What jobs are listed?”

Aethara answers: “Which employers are hiring aggressively right now?”

Aethara reads official employer ATS postings, removes duplicate reqs, tracks freshness, and scores hiring pressure so candidates know where to focus first.

Monster centers on searching job openings, uploading a resume, resume tools, and applying quickly — plus employer job posting, resume search, and recruitment solutions on the hiring side. Broad volume and resume utilities, not specialized hiring-pressure intelligence for employer-direct revenue roles.

Indeed, ZipRecruiter, and Google for Jobs gather postings from across the web at once. Indeed collects listings from many sources and de-duplicates them — but postings can still linger after a role is filled. That is massive discovery, not verified hiring pressure per req.

Glassdoor is strongest as a reviews, ratings, and workplace-sentiment product — valuable context about how people felt after they worked somewhere, not a live read of which sales teams are scaling tonight.

Workforce platforms like Revelio Labs estimate headcount and attrition by modeling millions of public profiles. Genuinely impressive for investors and enterprises — modeled data refreshed periodically, not employer ATS listings you can apply to tonight.

Aethara works the other way. Roles come directly from employers’ own applicant-tracking systems — such as Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby — timestamped, de-duplicated, scored for Hiring Aggressiveness, and linked to the official application. Not scraped from job boards. Not modeled from profiles. Sourced from the employer system.

What counts as ATS-direct

  • Apply URL resolves to the employer’s public ATS board (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable, and similar) — not a job-aggregator redirect.
  • Listing is tied to a live requisition id we track across refreshes; stale postings drop after 30 days.
  • No scraped Indeed/LinkedIn/Monster repost — we read the employer feed directly.
  • One canonical row per ATS requisition (multi-state clones collapse to a single listing + coverage list).
